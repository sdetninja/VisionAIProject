# Fire detected, immediate response
A confirmed fire is reported and handled under the site's emergency action plan (EAP), which must cover how fires are reported, the type of evacuation and exit routes, employees who shut down critical operations before leaving, accounting for all employees after evacuation, rescue and medical duties, and who to contact, 1910.38(c)(1)-(6). Sound the alarm, evacuate the affected zone and call the emergency response team. A fire hazard detected by a camera is a prompt to verify, not a replacement for the fire alarm system.

# Fire alarm system
The employer must have an employee alarm system with a distinctive signal for each purpose, 1910.38(d). Alarms must be noticed above ambient noise and light by everyone in the area and be recognisable as a signal to evacuate, 1910.165(b). Manual alarm stations are unobstructed, conspicuous and easy to reach, 1910.165(e). Non-supervised systems are tested every two months; when the system is out of service, backup methods such as runners or telephones are used, 1910.165(d).

# Evacuation and accounting for employees
Employees designated and trained under the EAP help others evacuate safely and in an orderly way, 1910.38(e). After evacuation every employee is accounted for, 1910.38(c)(4). The plan is reviewed with each employee when they start, when their duties change and when the plan changes, 1910.38(f). The plan is written and kept at the workplace; employers with 10 or fewer employees may communicate it orally, 1910.38(b).
