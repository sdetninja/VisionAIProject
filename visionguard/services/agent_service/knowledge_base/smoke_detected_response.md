title: Smoke detected, verification and response (based on US OSHA 29 CFR 1910.38, 1910.157 and 1910.252)
url: https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.38
note: Summary for decision support. OSHA has no smoke-specific rule; smoke is treated as a possible fire under the emergency action plan.

# Smoke detected, verify and report
Treat visible smoke as a possible fire until it is checked. Report it the way the emergency action plan says fires are reported, 1910.38(c)(1), and send a supervisor to verify in person. If flames, heat, a burning smell or spreading smoke are found, follow the fire response: sound the alarm and evacuate the zone, 1910.38. Camera smoke detection is weaker than fire detection; steam, dust, fog, exhaust and welding fumes can look like smoke, so every smoke alert needs human confirmation before action.

# Smoke from smouldering after hot work
Smoke detected near a recent welding or cutting job can come from smouldering material, a fire hazard that is easy to miss. Hot work rules require a fire watch for at least 30 minutes after welding or cutting ends, so smouldering fires are caught, 1910.252(a)(2)(iii)(B). Check the area, including below and behind the work, for hidden embers.

# Smoke at an incipient stage, extinguisher use
Only employees trained in extinguisher use and the hazards of incipient-stage fire fighting should attempt to put out a small fire, 1910.157(g). If the source is small, reachable and the person is trained, use the nearest suitable extinguisher; otherwise evacuate. If a false alarm is confirmed (steam, dust, welding arc), record it as a false positive so the detection can be improved.
